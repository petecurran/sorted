Is it fly-tipping? Say "no" if there is no dumped waste in the photo (a clean street, people, pets, food, a room indoors, a screen or a document), or if the waste has plainly been put out properly: wheelie bins, a skip, a building site, or bags set out neatly beside bins. Say "unsure" only if you really cannot tell.

Size, using the Environment Agency bands. First count the bags and the larger items in "items", then judge the whole pile:
- "Single black bag": one bag and nothing else.
- "Single item": one large item on its own, with nothing else or one bag: a sofa, a fridge freezer, a wardrobe, a footstool or a shopping trolley.
- "Car boot or less": what would fit in a family car boot. For example: 2 to 5 black bags; a mattress with a couple of bags; a carpet roll with 2 or 3 bags; a few paint tins and a bag of plaster; a small heap of garden cuttings with a bag or two.
- "Small van load": too much for a car, about 1 to 4 cubic metres. For example: 6 or more black bags; an armchair with 5 or more bags; a pile of bags, boxes and a broken chair spread along the pavement; a double mattress with a bed frame; a knee-high or waist-high heap of garden cuttings or rubble.
- "Transit van load": about 4 to 10 cubic metres. For example: 16 to 30 bags, several pieces of furniture with many bags, or a heap taller than a person.
- "Tipper lorry load" or "Significant/multiple loads": much more than that, usually on waste ground or a country lane.
When a pile is between two bands, choose by the number of bags: up to 5 is a car boot, 6 or more is a small van.

Waste type is what makes up most of the pile. A pile that is mainly black bags, even with some cardboard boxes or a broken chair, is "Black bags - household". A pile led by furniture, a mattress or a carpet roll, with a few bags beside it, is "Other household waste". Garden cuttings and hedge trimmings are "Green". Rubble, plasterboard, tiles and bags of plaster are "Construction/demolition/excavation". Fridges and freezers are "White goods". Fuel cans and oil drums are "Chemical drums, oil or fuel".

Land type: pavements, grass verges and roads are "Highway". "Footpath/bridleway" is only for paths away from roads, such as through a park. Garage courts are "Council land". Narrow lanes behind terraced houses are "Back alleyway".

Decision: "specialist" if the hazards could include asbestos, clinical waste, chemicals, paint or fuel. "hold_for_officer" if it is mostly household black bags, or trade waste that could contain documents. "not_a_fly_tip" if fly_tip is "no". Otherwise "clear_now".
