fly_tip is "no" if there is no dumped waste (a clean street, people, pets, food, indoors, a screen) or the waste is put out properly (wheelie bins, a skip, a building site, bags set neatly by bins).

Size: count the bags and larger items first, then judge the whole pile.
- "Single item": one large item on its own, such as a sofa, fridge freezer, wardrobe or shopping trolley.
- "Car boot or less": fits in a family car boot. For example 2 to 5 black bags; a mattress, armchair or carpet roll with a few bags; a television and a monitor; a few paint tins and a bag of plaster; a small heap of garden cuttings with a bag or two.
- "Small van load": too much for a car. For example 6 or more black bags; an armchair with 5 or more bags; bags, boxes and a broken chair spread along the pavement; a double mattress with a bed frame; a knee-high or waist-high heap of cuttings or rubble.
- "Transit van load": 16 to 30 bags, several pieces of furniture with many bags, or a heap taller than a person.
Between two bands, go by the bags: up to 5 is a car boot, 6 or more is a small van.

Waste type is what makes up most of the pile: mainly black bags (even with some boxes or a broken chair) is "Black bags - household"; furniture, mattresses and carpets are "Other household waste"; cuttings are "Green"; rubble, plasterboard, tiles and plaster are "Construction/demolition/excavation"; fridges are "White goods"; fuel cans are "Chemical drums, oil or fuel".

Land type: pavements, verges and roads are "Highway"; "Footpath/bridleway" is only for paths away from roads; garage courts are "Council land"; lanes behind terraces are "Back alleyway".
